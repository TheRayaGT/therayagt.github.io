OEIS

Contributions to the Online Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences.

  1. A028347Oct 2026

    $a(n)=n^2-4$.

    Contributed property: For $n\ge 1$, $a(n+1)$ is the minimum number of non-overlapping rectangular tiles, with sides on grid lines, which can be placed on an $n^2\times n^2$ grid of unit squares so that each row and each column contains exactly one uncovered unit square. The case $n=45$, i.e. $a(46)=2112$, is Problem 6 of the 2025 International Mathematical Olympiad ($2025\times 2025$ grid).

  2. A396925Aug 2026

    Numbers $k$ which have a divisor $d$ such that $d^2<k$ and $\phi(d)=\phi(k/d)$, where $\phi$ denotes Euler's totient function.

  3. A195055Jul 2026

    Decimal expansion of $\dfrac{\pi^2}{3}$.

    Contributed property: equals \[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{H(n)}{T(n)},\] where $H(n)$ is the $n^{\text{th}}$ harmonic number and $T(n)$ is the $n^{\text{th}}$ triangular number.

  4. A062119Jul 2026

    $a(n)=n!\,(n-1)$.

    Contributed property: equals \[\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k+i}\right)^{-1}.\]

  5. A397310Jul 2026

    Decimal expansion of $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}\cos(\cos x)\;dx$.

  6. A069998Jul 2026

    Decimal expansion of $\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}$.

    Contributed property: equals $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(x^2)}{x^2}\;dx$.

  7. A039686May 2026

    Squares which are the concatenation of two nonzero squares.

    Contributed property: the sequence is infinite because it contains $49\cdot 10^{2i}$ for $i\ge 0$ since $(7\cdot 10^i)^2$ can be split into the initial 4 and $9\cdot 10^{2i}$ with square root $3\cdot 10^i$.

  8. A395338Apr 2026

    Primes whose square is a concatenation of exactly 2 distinct strictly increasing non-zero squares.

  9. A342316Feb 2026

    Decimal expansion of $\dfrac{\pi}{2}-\log 2$.

    Contributed property: equals \[\int_{1}^{\infty}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{x^2}\right)\;dx.\]

  10. A392834Jan 2026

    Time taken to get $n$ people from one side of a bridge to the other where (a) the only flashlight must be carried when crossing; (b) at most three people may cross at the same time; (c) a group crosses at the speed of the slowest member; and (d) the $k^{\text{th}}$ person requires $k$ seconds to cross the bridge.

    With Gerard Sayson
